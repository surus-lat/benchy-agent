QUESTION
ANTROPOLOGÍA
DOCTORADO
OPEN-ENDED
In the Peruvian southern Andes, ayni labour exchange persists in communities where most household income is now waged or remittance-based. Explain the competing accounts of why it persists, and describe the fieldwork that would distinguish them.
WHY THIS QUESTION
Models default to a nostalgia-and-tradition reading, or flatten ayni into generic reciprocity theory. They rarely treat it as an active claim on future labour, and almost never name the ethnographic evidence that would tell the accounts apart.
SOURCES
DOI
10.1111/jlca.12476
LAB
Diarios de campo, Cusco 2019–2023
RQ
Authored by Dra. Rosa Quispe · Antropología Social, FLACSO
REFERENCE ANSWER & RUBRIC
peer-reviewed
10 PTS
EXPERT ANSWER
Two accounts compete. The moral-economy reading treats ayni as a standing claim on future labour that money cannot discharge; the risk-pooling reading treats it as insurance against income volatility that persists because wages are seasonal and remittances unreliable. They diverge on what happens when cash is abundant: risk-pooling predicts decline, moral economy predicts substitution in kind. Longitudinal labour-diary tracking across a remittance shock, paired with dispute cases where an ayni debt was refused in cash, separates them.
